## What it is

Bauhaus was a German school of art, architecture, and design founded by Walter Gropius in 1919. It brought artistic study and practical workshops together, asking how design could respond to modern materials, production, and everyday life. The school changed locations and leadership before closing under Nazi pressure in 1933; its influence continued through the work of its teachers and students. Bauhaus is often reduced to primary colors and simple shapes, but its broader project joined form, craft, technology, and use. [MoMA, Bauhaus](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/bauhaus), [Bauhaus-Archiv, The Bauhaus](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/das_bauhaus/).

## Recognize and apply it

- **Purposeful form:** Shape and structure respond to how an object or message is used. Remove details that do not help the task, while retaining useful character.
- **Geometry and clear hierarchy:** Circles, squares, and simple alignments support direct communication. Use geometry to organize content rather than as decoration alone.
- **Materials and making:** Industrial materials and workshop processes can shape a design. Show material qualities honestly and make sure the result remains practical.

For a poster or hero, combine a strong geometric image with concise sans-serif typography and a clear reading order. A limited palette can help, but Bauhaus work was varied; do not treat red, yellow, and blue as a required formula.

## Historical examples

### 1. Marcel Breuer, *Club chair (model B3 / Wassily)* (1927–1928)

- **Institution and direct source:** The Museum of Modern Art, [collection record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/2851/).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** MoMA provides the object record and images. The record credits the chair as a gift of Herbert Bayer; consult MoMA’s licensing guidance before reproducing the image.
- **What I observe:** Bent tubular steel outlines a familiar chair while canvas forms its seat, back, and arms. The structure is visible, and the materials give the object a lighter appearance than upholstered club furniture.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Use a product silhouette with visible structural lines and simple labels that explain materials or function.

### 2. Marianne Brandt, *Tea Infuser and Strainer* (c. 1924)

- **Institution and direct source:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art, [collection record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/491299).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** The Met record supplies object details and an image. Check the record’s rights information before reuse.
- **What I observe:** The infuser combines a round body, a cross-shaped handle, and precise metalwork. The geometry serves handling and pouring as well as visual order.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Pair a close product image with small callouts that connect each visible feature to a practical benefit.
