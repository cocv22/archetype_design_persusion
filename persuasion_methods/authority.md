# Authority

[Home](../README.md)

## Mechanism

People may give more weight to guidance from a source they regard as knowledgeable and credible, especially when they cannot easily judge the subject themselves. Authority is persuasive when the expertise is relevant to the claim and supported by evidence; a title, uniform, or celebrity status alone does not prove a claim.

## When and how to use it

Use authority when customers need help understanding product materials, construction, or care. Name the expert, explain why their experience applies, and show the work or evidence. For a white T-shirt, imagery could show a textile specialist examining the fabric; use clear labels, a restrained palette, and readable typography so credentials and product information stay legible. Keep sponsorships and employment relationships visible.

## Example

**Headline:** Built for everyday wear.

**Supporting copy:** “Mina Patel, our production manager with 12 years in garment construction, explains how the double-stitched shoulder seam is made.” Link to construction details and identify her role.

**CTA and destination:** “See how it's made” → a page with seam photos, materials, and the expert's explanation.

**Why the mechanism is present:** The claim is explained by a named person with directly relevant experience, and the customer can inspect the product evidence.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not invent an expert, exaggerate credentials, imply independent testing that did not occur, or use a person’s expertise to support a claim outside their field. Disclose paid endorsements and keep evidence accessible. Authority should help customers assess a claim, not discourage questions.
