## What it is

Art Deco is an international design movement that became prominent in the 1920s and 1930s. It brought together streamlined geometry, rich materials, strong color, and decorative pattern across architecture, interiors, furniture, fashion, and graphic design. The name is associated with the 1925 Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs et Industriels Modernes in Paris. Art Deco designers drew on diverse sources, including Cubism, ancient art, and modern technology; the style is not simply a synonym for luxury or gold decoration. [V&A, An introduction to Art Deco](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/an-introduction-to-art-deco), [The Met, French Art Deco](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/french-art-deco).

## Recognize and apply it

- **Geometric ornament:** Zigzags, stepped forms, sunbursts, and repeated lines create rhythm. Select a small set of motifs so ornament supports the main message.
- **Streamlined silhouettes:** Symmetry, vertical emphasis, and clean contours suggest movement and modernity. Let the silhouette remain recognizable at a glance.
- **Material and color contrast:** Polished metal, glass, lacquer, and vivid or dark colors can create richness. In digital design, use contrast and texture sparingly to keep information legible.

For a poster or hero, combine one stepped frame or repeating geometric pattern with a strong title and restrained metallic or jewel-tone accents. Keep the product or offer central instead of relying on ornament alone.

## Historical examples

### 1. Émile-Jacques Ruhlmann, *Drouant Side Chair* (c. 1925)

- **Institution and direct source:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art, [collection record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/487426).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** The Met record identifies the chair and provides collection imagery. Check the record’s rights information before reproducing the image.
- **What I observe:** Macassar ebony, silvered bronze, and silk upholstery bring material contrast to a compact, measured form. The chair shows how Art Deco could combine geometry and fine craft.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Use one polished material cue and a precise frame around the product, reserving the strongest contrast for the headline.

### 2. Jean Dunand, *Vase* (c. 1925)

- **Institution and direct source:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art, [collection record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/487038).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** The Met record gives the object details and image. It identifies Open Access materials where applicable; confirm the record’s rights statement before reuse.
- **What I observe:** Vivid geometric patterning emphasizes the vase’s rounded form. The contrast between a simple silhouette and richly patterned surface captures Art Deco’s mix of modern shape and ornament.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Pair a simple product silhouette with a single geometric pattern, keeping the headline and offer on a quieter field.
