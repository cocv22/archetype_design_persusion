# Liking

[Home](../README.md)

## Mechanism

People are often more receptive to requests from someone they like. Warmth, familiarity, shared interests, and sincere cooperation can contribute to liking. A friendly presentation may make a brand easier to engage with, but it does not establish that a product or claim is trustworthy.

## When and how to use it

Use a warm, approachable tone when helping customers compare options or ask questions. Show real people and relevant situations, with permission, and avoid stereotypes. For a white T-shirt, customer fit photos and conversational, readable copy can help shoppers picture how the shirt might work for them. Keep the price, fit, and product facts just as clear as the friendly message.

## Example

**Headline:** Your everyday tee, your way.

**Supporting copy:** “Some people like a closer fit; others prefer room through the shoulders. Compare the garment measurements or ask us to help you choose.” Show customers with different fits and styles, accurately captioned.

**CTA and destination:** “Compare fits” → a size guide with measurements and customer fit notes.

**Why the mechanism is present:** The brand speaks in a welcoming way and recognizes different customer preferences, making the interaction feel considerate rather than transactional.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not use false intimacy, manipulative flattery, or stereotypes to manufacture a connection. Avoid claiming a personal relationship that does not exist or using friendliness to hide fees, limitations, or a sales motive. People should be able to judge the offer on its facts.
