# Commitment and Consistency

[Home](../README.md)

## Mechanism

People often prefer actions that fit choices or commitments they have already made. A small, voluntary step can make a related next step feel natural. An earlier action is not consent to a later purchase, and people must be able to change their minds.

## When and how to use it

Use this principle to support a customer-chosen process, such as saving fit preferences for later. Make each step optional and explain its consequences. A clear progress indicator, simple buttons, and consistent colors can help customers follow the process; keep “skip,” “edit,” and cancellation controls easy to find. Do not make a sequence look mandatory when it is not.

## Example

**Headline:** Find the fit you prefer.

**Supporting copy:** “Compare the garment measurements, choose a fit, and save your preference for next time. Saving is optional and does not add the shirt to your cart.”

**CTA and destination:** “Save my fit” → a preference page with options to edit or remove the saved choice.

**Why the mechanism is present:** The customer makes a small, explicit choice that can make future comparisons easier, while remaining in control of whether to continue.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not treat a quiz, sample request, or saved preference as consent to buy, subscribe, or receive marketing. Avoid preselected boxes, hidden recurring charges, guilt-based reminders, and confusing cancellation flows. Make commitments specific and revisable.
