# Social Proof

[Home](../README.md)

## Mechanism

People may look to others' choices and behavior for clues, especially in an unfamiliar situation. Reviews and examples of use can reduce uncertainty. Popularity does not prove quality or establish that a product is right for everyone.

## When and how to use it

Use authentic, current customer feedback when it helps people understand practical details. Show the number of reviews behind a rating, dates, relevant use context, and a range of opinions. Customer photos need permission; label incentives clearly. Use a clean, legible design so ratings and product information can be evaluated together.

## Example

**Headline:** See how the fit works for other customers.

**Supporting copy:** “Rated 4.6 out of 5 by 218 verified purchasers. Reviewers share their usual size and preferred fit; see all reviews, including critical feedback.” Use only accurate, up-to-date figures.

**CTA and destination:** “Read fit reviews” → a review section with dates, verified-purchase labels, and size information.

**Why the mechanism is present:** Shoppers can learn from real customers' experiences in a relevant context instead of relying only on the seller's claims.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not fabricate reviews or customer counts, suppress criticism, or present paid testimonials without disclosure. Do not use stock photos as if they show customers. Explain how ratings are calculated and avoid claiming that popularity guarantees suitability.
