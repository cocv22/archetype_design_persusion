# Constructivism

[Home](README.md)

## What it is

Constructivism developed within the Russian avant-garde around the 1917 October Revolution. Artists and designers explored how art might take part in a transformed society, moving toward construction, material, and practical communication. Constructivist work includes sculpture, typography, photography, book design, and posters; it was not one fixed visual recipe. Aleksandr Rodchenko and Varvara Stepanova were among the artists who applied its ideas to graphic and applied design. [MoMA, Constructivism](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/constructivism), [MoMA, Rodchenko](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/16012).

## Recognize and apply it

- **Dynamic diagonals:** Angled forms create movement and urgency. Use one dominant direction to guide attention rather than making every element compete.
- **Type as image:** Large, geometric lettering can carry both information and visual force. Keep essential copy readable at its intended size.
- **Photomontage and contrast:** Photography, shape, and type can be combined into direct visual statements. Use images with permission and ensure the combination supports the message.

For a campaign poster, make one image or headline dominant, then use a strong diagonal and concise supporting information to direct the viewer toward the action.

## Historical examples

### 1. Aleksandr Rodchenko, *Books (Please)! In All Branches of Knowledge* (1924; poster reproduction 1967)

- **Institution and direct source:** Frye Art Museum, [collection record](https://fryemuseum.org/artwork/books-please-2015.010S.30).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** The Frye record documents a reproduction of the 1924 poster, printed in 1967, and credits the photograph to Mark Woods. Follow the museum’s rights guidance before reusing its image.
- **What I observe:** A sharply cropped face appears to shout into a red geometric form, with lettering arranged as part of the action. The composition makes its promotional purpose immediate.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Use an original close crop and one directional shape to point toward the offer, with a short headline that remains readable.

### 2. Aleksandr Rodchenko, *Pro eto. Ei i mne (About This. To Her and to Me)* (1923)

- **Institution and direct source:** The Museum of Modern Art, [collection record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/16012).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** The record describes the book and shows collection imagery. Check MoMA’s image licensing terms before reproducing any page or cover.
- **What I observe:** Rodchenko’s photomontage and structured typography form a visual counterpart to Vladimir Mayakovsky’s poem. The book design combines image and text as a composed sequence.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Combine an original photograph and short typographic message, using scale and crop to create emphasis without obscuring the information.
