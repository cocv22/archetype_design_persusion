# Reciprocity

[Home](../README.md)

## Mechanism

People often feel inclined to respond to a favor or useful gesture with something in return. A sample, service, or practical resource can create goodwill and help customers assess an offer. The benefit should have value even if the customer chooses not to buy.

## When and how to use it

Offer something genuinely useful before asking for a sale: a fabric swatch, sizing guide, or repair instructions. Show what is included and state any fees or conditions clearly. Use inviting product imagery, readable terms, and direct phrasing. If personal information is required, explain why and how it will be used.

## Example

**Headline:** Feel the fabric before you choose.

**Supporting copy:** “Request a free cotton swatch to check the fabric's weight and texture. No purchase required. One swatch per household; shipping is free.”

**CTA and destination:** “Request a swatch” → a short form explaining what information is collected and why.

**Why the mechanism is present:** The brand provides a useful product sample that reduces uncertainty, without requiring a purchase or favorable review in return.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not describe a forced sign-up or hidden charge as a free gift. Do not imply that customers owe a purchase, personal data, or positive review in return for a sample. Keep eligibility limits and shipping terms prominent.
