# Postmodern Graphic Design

[Home](README.md)

## What it is

Postmodern graphic design includes approaches from the late 1970s onward that question the idea of one neutral, universal visual language. Designers drew on historical typography, popular culture, expressive composition, layered images, and emerging digital tools. New Wave typography is one strand: it responds to strict modernist grid systems by making spacing, type, and image relationships more varied while still using deliberate structure. [MoMA, April Greiman’s *Does It Make Sense?*](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/172729), [V&A, What is Postmodernism?](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-postmodernism).

## Recognize and apply it

- **Expressive typography:** Type may vary in scale, weight, spacing, and orientation. Keep body text and calls to action easy to read.
- **Layering and visible composition:** Text and imagery can overlap, shift, or interrupt a strict grid. Use hierarchy so the viewer knows where to look first.
- **Historical and digital references:** Older typefaces and print textures can sit beside pixelation, scan effects, or digital imagery. Choose references that suit the subject and avoid effects that make information inaccessible.

For a poster or hero, let one typographic gesture create character, then protect a clear reading path from headline to supporting copy to CTA.

## Historical examples

### 1. April Greiman, *Does It Make Sense?*, *Design Quarterly* no. 133 (1986)

- **Institution and direct source:** The Museum of Modern Art, [collection record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/172729).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** MoMA provides the two-sided foldout design as a viewing image. The record identifies the publisher as Walker Art Center and credits the designer; MoMA requires permission through its licensing partners for reproduction. Link for viewing unless licensed.
- **What I observe:** Greiman layers digital imagery, text, and textures in a long, spatial composition. The piece adopts early computer graphics as a design vocabulary instead of treating the computer as a neutral production tool.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Use a cropped digital texture and one scale shift in the headline, while keeping the CTA on a clean, high-contrast panel.

### 2. Paula Scher, *The Diva is Dismissed* (1994)

- **Institution and direct source:** The Museum of Modern Art, [collection record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/8837).
- **Image/viewing, credit, and reuse:** MoMA provides a viewing image and records the work as a 1994 serigraph, gifted by the designer. The record identifies copyright restrictions; link to view and obtain permission before reproducing it.
- **What I observe:** Scher uses unorthodox spacing, varied type weights, and historical typefaces to build an energetic hierarchy. The eclectic typography joins high and popular culture, a postmodern alternative to restrained modernist typography.
- **How I would adapt it in a hero:** Combine two contrasting type styles for emphasis, then limit the variation to the headline so supporting information stays legible.
