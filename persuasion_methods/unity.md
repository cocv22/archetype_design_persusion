# Unity

[Home](../README.md)

## Mechanism

Unity is based on shared identity or belonging: people may be more receptive to someone they experience as part of “us.” Unity is distinct from simply liking someone; it comes from meaningful group membership, shared experience, or participation. A brand cannot create genuine unity by declaring that everyone belongs.

## When and how to use it

Use unity when there is a real connection to a community, shared practice, or common experience. Let community members shape how they are represented and use group symbols only with permission. For a white T-shirt, imagery could show actual members at a gathering; use colors and language drawn from the group's real context, and make the shirt optional rather than a condition of belonging.

## Example

**Headline:** Made for the Saturday morning ride.

**Supporting copy:** “Designed with the Riverside Cycling Club for the rides we share. Club members helped choose the print; joining the club does not require buying a shirt.” Show members participating in an actual ride, with permission.

**CTA and destination:** “See the club design” → a page explaining the collaboration and how to join a ride.

**Why the mechanism is present:** The message points to a real shared activity and member participation, rather than claiming that a purchase creates group membership.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not fabricate belonging, speak for a community without its involvement, or use group symbols without permission. Avoid “real locals wear this” and other membership tests. Do not frame outsiders as inferior or make a purchase the price of belonging.
